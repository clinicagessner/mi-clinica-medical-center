You shaved your beard close for an interview, and the next morning your neck was covered in red dots that sting against your collar. Or you ran a razor over your legs before a wedding and now there are itchy bumps right where your pants rub. It's one of the most common skin complaints among adults in Spring Branch and across Houston, and it usually goes by two names: **ingrown hairs** and **folliculitis**. Both tend to be mild, but they're worth recognizing, because every so often one of those small bumps turns into something that won't heal on its own.

## Two problems that look alike at first glance

Every hair grows out of a tiny pocket under the skin called a follicle. The trouble can start with the hair itself or with that pocket.

- **Ingrown hair:** as the hair grows, it curls back and pushes into the skin instead of coming out. Your body treats it like a splinter and the area around it swells. It happens far more with curly or coarse hair, and especially after a very close shave or waxing.
- **Folliculitis:** this is inflammation or infection of the follicle, often from bacteria that already live on your skin and take advantage of a tiny nick, like the kind a blade leaves. It looks like a rash of small red bumps, sometimes with a white tip, around several hairs at once.

In real life the two often show up together: an ingrown hair irritates the follicle, and the follicle ends up infected. This table sums up how each one usually looks and what to do about it:

| | Ingrown hair | Folliculitis | Boil or abscess |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it looks like | Red bump with a trapped hair or a dark shadow under it | Several small bumps, sometimes white-tipped, in a hairy area | Larger, deeper, warm lump that hurts more each day |
| How it feels | Mild itch or sting | Itching and burning | Throbbing pain, sometimes fever |
| What to do | Stop shaving the area for a while, care at home | Keep it clean, don't shave over it, keep an eye on it | Get it checked at the clinic; don't squeeze it |

## Area by area: why it shows up where it does

### Beard and neck

This is the classic spot for men with curly hair. A razor leaves each hair with a sharp, angled tip, and that tip bends around and pokes back into the skin of the neck or jaw. Shaving against the grain, pulling the skin tight as you shave, or going over the same patch again and again all make it worse.

### Armpits

Three things meet here: thick hair, sweat and constant rubbing from your arm and your clothes. In hot jobs, like construction or a restaurant kitchen, your underarms stay damp for hours: just what bacteria like.

### Legs, groin and buttocks

On the legs it usually follows shaving or waxing, and it gets worse with leggings, tight jeans or workout clothes that stay soaked with sweat. In the groin and buttocks the friction is even greater, which is why that's where a small bump most often turns into a lump full of pus.

### After a hot tub or a pool

There's a type of folliculitis that shows up after a soak in a hot tub or a swim in a poorly chlorinated pool: itchy bumps appear, mostly where your swimsuit covered the skin. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/) links it to a germ that grows in warm water that isn't properly treated. A soapy shower afterward and not sitting around in a wet swimsuit help.

## What actually helps at home

For most mild cases, these simple steps are enough:

- **Give the area a break:** stop shaving or waxing there while you have bumps.
- **Warm compresses:** a clean washcloth soaked in warm water, held on the area a few times a day, eases the swelling and helps a trapped hair work its way out.
- **Wash with water and a mild soap**, then pat dry instead of rubbing hard with the towel.
- **Loose, cotton clothing** while it heals, and change out of sweaty clothes as soon as you can.
- **When you go back to shaving:** wet skin and hair, shaving gel or soap, a clean sharp blade, with the direction the hair grows and no second pass.
- **Don't share** razors, towels or loofahs.

## What makes it worse

What does the most damage: digging hairs out with tweezers or needles, squeezing bumps with your fingernails, dabbing on rubbing alcohol or home remedies that burn, and shaving right over inflamed skin. If a new hair-removal cream or deodorant left the whole area red, itchy or covered in welts, it may be a skin reaction rather than folliculitis; we look at those reactions under our [allergy care](/en/services/alergias).

## When a bump becomes a boil or an abscess

Sometimes the infection in the follicle goes deeper. That's a **boil**: a red lump that starts firm and then softens, warm to the touch and more painful every day. When pus collects under the skin, it's called an **abscess**.

These clues tell you it's no longer an ordinary ingrown hair:

- The lump keeps growing instead of shrinking with home care.
- It hurts when you walk, raise your arm or sit down.
- It feels soft, as if there's fluid inside, or a yellow tip appears.
- The skin surrounding the lump looks flushed and feels hot when you touch it.
- You run a fever or feel generally unwell.

That kind of lump shouldn't be squeezed. ## When the bumps keep coming back

When flare-ups or boils keep returning, the usual reasons are hair type and shaving technique, everyday friction and sweat, and the bacteria that live on the skin. There's also a cause you can't see: high blood sugar. If abscesses keep returning, the medical team may suggest checking your glucose with a [blood test](/en/services/examenes-sangre), especially if diabetes runs in your family.

## Signs that mean the ER

**Call 911 or go to the emergency room** if you notice any of these:

- Red skin that spreads quickly, along with fever or chills.
- Red streaks running from the bump toward the groin or armpit.
- A lump on the face, especially near the eye or nose, that swells a lot.
- After putting a product on your skin, your lips, tongue or face swell up, or breathing gets hard.
- Confusion, severe weakness or dizziness.
