## What Is Vitamin B12 and What Does It Do?

Vitamin B12 is an essential nutrient your body needs for basic functions:

- **Making red blood cells** — the cells that carry oxygen throughout your body
- **Protecting the nervous system** — it maintains myelin, the layer that coats your nerves
- **Producing energy** — it's part of how your body turns food into fuel
- **Supporting memory and focus** — your brain needs it to work well
- **Building DNA** — the genetic material of every new cell

Here's the key detail: your body **does not produce** vitamin B12. You only get it from animal-based foods or supplements. So when your diet falls short or your body can't absorb it well, your level drops slowly without you noticing.

## Benefits of a Healthy B12 Level

When your vitamin B12 is where it should be, you feel it:

- More energy and less fatigue during the day
- Better mood — B12 plays a role in serotonin production
- A clearer mind: better memory and concentration
- Prevention of B12-deficiency anemia
- Protected nerves: less tingling and numbness
- Overall metabolic support

Let's be clear, though: vitamin B12 is **not a magic energy booster and it does not help you lose weight**. If your level is already normal, taking more won't give you "super energy." The real benefit is correcting or preventing a deficiency — and the first step is always measuring it with a [blood test](/en/services/examenes-sangre).

## Symptoms of Vitamin B12 Deficiency

Deficiency develops slowly, and its signs are easy to mistake for stress or ordinary tiredness. Pay attention if you notice:

- Fatigue or weakness that doesn't improve with rest
- Tingling or numbness in your hands and feet
- Pale or slightly yellowish skin
- A swollen, red or burning tongue
- Memory problems or trouble concentrating
- Mood changes: irritability, sadness or low motivation
- Dizziness or shortness of breath with exertion
- Balance problems when walking

If several of these sound familiar, don't ignore them: prolonged deficiency can cause permanent nerve damage. Detecting it takes one simple blood test.

## Who Is Most at Risk of Deficiency?

Anyone can run low, but the risk goes up if:

- **You're over 50** — with age the stomach produces less acid, and without it B12 isn't absorbed well
- **You take omeprazole or other antacids long-term** — they reduce the acid needed to absorb it
- **You take metformin for diabetes** — this very common medication lowers B12 absorption with long-term use; if you have diabetes or another [chronic condition](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas), it's worth checking your level at least once a year
- **You follow a vegetarian or vegan diet** — natural B12 only comes from animal-based foods
- **You've had stomach or bariatric surgery** — the body loses its ability to absorb it
- **You have chronic gastritis, celiac disease or Crohn's** — an inflamed gut absorbs less
- **You drink alcohol in excess** — it irritates the stomach and interferes with absorption

Diabetes is especially common in our Hispanic community, and many patients have taken metformin for years without anyone ever checking their vitamin B12. If that's you, ask us at your next visit.

## Foods Rich in Vitamin B12

A varied diet helps maintain your level. The best sources are:

- **Beef liver** — the most concentrated source of all
- **Fish** such as salmon, tuna, trout and sardines — high content
- **Beef and chicken** — moderate to high content
- **Eggs** — especially the yolk
- **Dairy** — milk, cheese and yogurt
- **Fortified cereals** — a useful option for vegetarians (check the label)

One key point: if your problem is **absorption** (due to age, medications, surgery or intestinal disease), eating well isn't enough. The vitamin passes through your stomach but never reaches your blood. In those cases you need supplementation — and often, the injection.

## Injection or Pills? Which One Is Right for You

Both options have their place, and the choice depends on your case:

- **Pills or oral supplements** — work well for mild deficiency or maintenance, when your intestine absorbs normally
- **Intramuscular injection** — the right choice when the deficiency is moderate or severe, when neurological symptoms are already present (tingling, numbness, balance problems) or when there's an absorption problem. The injection goes straight into your bloodstream, bypassing the stomach and intestine entirely

## How Often Is the B12 Injection Given?

There's no one-size-fits-all schedule: the right frequency comes from your blood work — it's never given "blindly." In general, the plan works like this:

- **Correction phase** — weekly injections during the first few weeks, to raise your level quickly
- **Maintenance phase** — one monthly injection, depending on how you respond and what caused the deficiency
